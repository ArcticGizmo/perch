Perch · overlay concepts

Usage across orgs

Perch's usage bars assume one account. With a config directory per org, each carries its own four readouts — Session 5h, Weekly 7d, Fable F and Credits $. Ways to show that in a slim overlay — drag the slider to watch each cope, or buckle, as the orgs pile up.

Stacked blocks

≤ 3 orgs

Today's bars, once per org: every metric labelled and full-width. Nothing to learn.

Buckles: three bars per org — the panel grows unbounded and eats the overlay.

Tabs

many

A tab strip of orgs; the selected one shows full bars. Pips flag orgs needing a look.

Trade: constant height, but you only see one org's budget at a time.

Headroom rings

many

One ring per org, filled and coloured by its worst metric. A wrap-around grid.

Trade: instant "who's hot", but a ring hides which limit and the exact spend.

Heat matrix

many

Orgs down, metrics across; each cell coloured by its own severity. Densest option.

Trade: a whole estate at a glance — but it reads as a spreadsheet, not a gauge.

Attention list

any

Only orgs nearing a limit, worst first; the calm ones fold into a single tally.

Trade: scales to any count and leads with risk — but hides steady-state totals.

Dense table

many

One line per org, sorted hottest first: a mini 5h bar, then 7d, F and spend.

Trade: precise and compact; the sparkbars are small and there's a lot to scan.

Variations you asked for

Concentric rings with an on-pace tick, vertical micro-bars capped to a two-letter code, and tabs that preview every org under its header. Same slider drives these too.

Concentric rings

many

All four metrics as nested rings per org — 5h, 7d, F, $, outside in. The tick marks where an even burn would sit; fill past it means running hot.

Trade: pace-aware and complete, but four rings in one glyph asks the eye to work.

Vertical micro-bars

many

Each org is a tiny four-bar column — 5h, 7d, F, $ — beneath a two-letter code. An equaliser of orgs.

Trade: very compact and wraps wide; two letters can collide (initrode vs initech).

Tabs + preview lines

many

The tab approach, but every header carries a mini headroom line — read all orgs at a glance, open one for full bars.

Trade: the best of both worlds, at the cost of a taller tab strip.

Pace-first ideas

You called the expected-rate mark the valuable part — so here are four where the actual-vs-expected gap is the visualisation, not a tick added on top.

Pace-marker bars

≤ 4 orgs

Today's bar — fill for used, a bright line for the expected pace — kept exactly, one org per block.

Trade: the most faithful to what you have; still grows tall with many orgs.

Divergence from pace

any

Only the gap: a bar left of centre is under pace, right is over. Sorted most-over first.

Trade: "who's ahead of budget" leaps out; it drops the absolute fill level.

Expected vs now

many

A lollipop per org: hollow dot where an even burn would sit, filled dot where you actually are.

Trade: expected and actual read as one comparison; shown for the 5-hour window.

Burn trajectory

many

Your slope against the even-burn diagonal. Steeper than the dashes and you hit the cap early.

Trade: projects overspend, not just current state; needs a moment to read.

Notes for building it

LeaningKeep the expected-rate mark front and centre: a divergence-from-pace list to triage many orgs at once, opening a full pace-marker bar for the one you're working in.

Single dir is unchangedNone of this shows unless more than one config directory is in play — exactly how the directory chips already gate.

Polling costEach org means another /api/oauth/usage call against its own token. Stagger the cadence; the endpoint throttles.

macOS caveatTokens live per-dir on Windows/Linux, but in one shared Keychain on macOS — per-org usage there needs a spike first.
